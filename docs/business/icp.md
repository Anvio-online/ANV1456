# Ideal Customer Profile

**Status:** v1 · Living version of `docs/Initial/Brand_strategy.md` §1–§2.

---

## 1. Primary ICP

**Small and medium businesses, 10–200 employees.**

Qualifying characteristics — a good fit usually has three or more:

- Still runs core operations manually (spreadsheets, email threads, copy-paste between systems)
- Wants a modern website or web application and knows the current one is holding them back
- Interested in AI but has **no idea where to start** — this phrase, or its equivalent, is the strongest single signal
- Has a person who owns the problem and can sign (founder, ops head, marketing head)
- Revenue exists. This is not a startup betting a pre-seed round on us

**Geography:** India first. **UAE is the deliberate second market** — Dubai/Abu Dhabi SMBs, ecommerce and professional services especially.

---

## 2. Secondary ICP

- **Startups building AI products** — they need engineering capacity, not education
- **International clients beyond UAE** — opportunistic, not targeted

---

## 3. Explicitly not the target

**Enterprise.** Not because we can't deliver — because the sales cycle is 6–18 months and Anvio cannot fund that wait today. Revisit when there's runway, not before.

Also decline, or price to decline:

- WordPress-theme-installation work
- Pure graphic design, brand identity with no build attached
- Social-media-management retainers with no technical component
- Anyone whose actual ask is "the cheapest possible website"

Each of these is off-position per [positioning.md](positioning.md) §4. Taking them makes Anvio harder to describe, which is the expensive part.

---

## 4. Who the buyer actually is

**Non-technical decision-maker.** They do not know what n8n is, do not care what model powers a chatbot, and will not read an architecture diagram.

Consequences that bind every agent and every page:

- Lead with the **business outcome**, never the stack. "Your team stops re-typing invoices" beats "RAG pipeline with vector retrieval."
- Quantify in **hours and rupees**, not in tokens, latency, or accuracy percentages.
- The stack is a **credibility artifact, shown after trust**, not an opener.
- They are risk-averse about AI specifically. Address data handling early and concretely — the `/privacy` page exists partly for this.

---

## 5. Buying triggers worth watching for

The moments that turn "interested someday" into a conversation:

| Trigger                                                 | What to say                                        |
| ------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| Hiring for a role that is mostly data entry             | The role costs more than automating it             |
| Just raised, or just crossed a growth threshold         | Manual process is about to break                   |
| Website visibly outdated, or slow, or not mobile-usable | Lead loss is measurable                            |
| A competitor shipped something visibly better           | Specific, urgent, and time-boxed                   |
| Announced expansion to a new market/region              | New site, new content, new automation, all at once |
| Complaining publicly about support volume               | Chatbot / triage automation                        |

Lead research and sales intelligence agents should hunt for these explicitly, not just for firmographics.

---

## 6. Disqualifiers

Score a lead down hard, or drop it, when:

- No identifiable decision-maker
- Fewer than ~10 employees **and** no revenue signal — usually can't afford the floor in [services-and-pricing.md](services-and-pricing.md)
- Enterprise procurement is visible (vendor portals, RFP processes)
- The only stated need is design, or the only stated need is "SEO" with no build
- They already have an in-house engineering team of any real size and no stated gap

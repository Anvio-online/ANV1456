## 1. Who Anvio is actually compared against

Not "other AI agencies." A prospect's real alternatives, in the order they consider them:

1. **Do nothing.** The most common competitor by far, and the one most pitches ignore.
2. **Hire someone in-house.** Loses on time-to-value and total cost; wins on control.
3. **A cheap freelancer or a WordPress shop.** Wins on price. This is who the ₹ floors exist to lose against, deliberately.
4. **A local full-service digital agency.** Wins on relationship and breadth; usually cannot do real AI/automation.
5. **A specialist automation consultancy.** Wins on automation depth; usually cannot build the software or the growth layer.
6. **A DIY no-code attempt.** Wins on cost; the failure mode is a half-finished workflow nobody owns.

## 2. Where the differentiator actually bites

Against each alternative, the four-under-one-roof claim from [positioning.md](positioning.md) §2 cashes out differently:

| Against                | The line that lands                                                  |
| ---------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Do nothing             | Quantified cost of the status quo — hours/month, in rupees           |
| In-house hire          | One salary vs. one project, and delivery starting this month         |
| Cheap freelancer       | Ownership, documentation, and someone still here in 30 days          |
| Local agency           | They can build a site; can they connect it to your operations?       |
| Automation consultancy | They can automate it; can they build the thing being automated?      |
| DIY no-code            | Who owns it when it breaks and the person who built it has moved on? |

**Never name a competitor in client-facing copy.** Compete on the axis, not the brand.

## 4. Honest read on our own weaknesses

Naming these makes every plan better, and hiding them makes every plan wrong:

- **No client proof under the Anvio name**, and none at all in the category we lead with
- **Zero domain authority** — invisible in search for at least two more quarters
- **One part-time person** — real delivery-capacity ceiling, and a real bus factor
- **No published automation pricing** — a friction point competitors with rate cards don't have
- **No analytics** — we cannot currently measure whether anything is working

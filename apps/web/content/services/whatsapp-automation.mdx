Most small businesses run some part of their sales and support through WhatsApp already. The problem isn't the channel — it's that a person has to be the one reading, typing, and remembering, for every single message.

## What WhatsApp automation actually is

It's software that reads incoming WhatsApp messages, decides what to do with them, and acts — replying, logging the order in your CRM, or handing off to a person with the context already attached. It runs on the **WhatsApp Business API**, not the regular WhatsApp Business app you'd install on a phone. That distinction matters more than it sounds: the regular app has no way for software to read or send anything. The API is what makes automation possible at all, and getting Meta's business verification for it is usually the first real step in any project — it can take a few days, so it's worth starting before anything else.

## What it actually replaces

Concretely, three things:

1. **Order capture.** A customer messages their order in free text or a photo of a list. The automation reads it, structures it, and creates or updates the record in your CRM — no one re-typing it from the chat window.
2. **First-line replies.** "Are you open," "where's my order," "do you deliver to \[area\]" — answered instantly, from your actual policies and current order data, not a static FAQ that goes stale.
3. **Follow-up timing.** A lead that goes quiet for two days gets a scheduled follow-up automatically, instead of depending on someone remembering to scroll back through yesterday's chats.

## How it works

  The 24-hour rule below is the single most common thing that trips up a first WhatsApp automation
  project — worth reading closely if you're new to the Business API.
</Callout>

The flow for a typical message looks like this: the message arrives, the automation checks whether it can answer confidently from your policies and order data, and either replies directly or hands off to a person with the conversation and its own read on the situation attached. Nothing gets silently dropped — if it can't answer, a human sees it, with context, not a blank ping.

There's one structural constraint that shapes every WhatsApp automation: you can only send a **free-form reply** within 24 hours of the customer's last message. Outside that window — a delivery reminder three days later, a re-engagement message to a lead gone cold — you can only send a **pre-approved template message**, reviewed by Meta in advance. This isn't a limitation we work around; it's why order confirmations, shipping updates, and reminders are built as templates from the start rather than retrofitted later when the free-form window has already closed.

After-hours messages don't wait for the next business day. The automation handles what it confidently can at 11pm the same way it would at 11am, and queues anything genuinely needing a person for the morning — with nothing lost in between.

## What it costs and how long it takes

|                                   | Typical range                                                                   |
| --------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Build cost                        | ₹25,000–₹80,000, depending on how many flows (orders, support, follow-ups)      |
| Timeline to first live automation | 2–4 weeks                                                                       |
| Meta's per-conversation fee       | Varies by category and market — confirmed with you before launch, not estimated |

The build cost depends mainly on scope — how many distinct flows you want handled — not on how many messages you send. Meta charges separately, per conversation, once you're live; that fee changes periodically and is genuinely Meta's to set, so we confirm the current rate with you rather than quote a number here that might already be stale by the time you read this.

## What it doesn't do

It doesn't replace a person who needs to make a judgement call — a genuine complaint, a custom quote, a situation your policies don't cover. Those are exactly the cases it's built to recognize and hand off, not attempt. It also doesn't work if your policies and pricing live only in someone's head — the automation is only as good as what it can actually reference, so part of the build is usually making that information explicit for the first time, which tends to be useful on its own.

It's also worth being direct about the difference between this and a chatbot: this page is about **acting** on a message — logging an order, updating a record. If what you need is mainly **answering** questions accurately from documentation, that's [AI chatbot development](/services/automate/ai-chatbot-development) — a related but distinct service, and the two are often used together.

## What you own afterward

The workflow, the integration code, and the documentation are yours. If you want to hand it to an in-house developer later, or move to a different vendor, nothing here is built to lock you in — that's a deliberate position, not a courtesy.
